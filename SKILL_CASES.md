# Skill Case Studies

These are sanitized design notes. They explain why certain private skills exist without publishing the actual skill files, prompts, rubrics, examples, or source materials.

## 1. LinkedIn Person Prep

Problem:

Preparing for a networking conversation should not require 30 minutes of manual tabs. Generic web fetching is also unreliable for LinkedIn-style people research, especially when the useful context is split across a person's profile, their company, recent role changes, and the user's own meeting goal.

Why a skill made sense:

The recurring job was not "summarize this profile." The real job was:

- understand who this person is
- understand what their company actually does
- infer what topics are likely worth discussing
- generate thoughtful questions that sound specific without pretending to know too much
- avoid generic networking scripts

What the private skill handles:

- routes to a configured MCP-style connector or other appropriate source when available
- separates person-level context from company-level context
- looks for credible connection points
- turns the research into conversation-ready questions
- keeps the output short enough to use before a call

Public takeaway:

This skill exists because the workflow is repeated, high-context, and time-sensitive. It saves time not by writing a better generic prompt, but by standardizing how context is gathered, filtered, and turned into a useful conversation plan.

## 2. Company Research

Problem:

Company websites, job posts, funding announcements, and founder profiles all describe the same company from different angles. Manually combining them is slow, and a generic AI summary often misses the real decision: whether the company is worth targeting and how to position yourself.

Why a skill made sense:

The recurring job was not "tell me about this company." The real job was:

- translate what the company actually does
- identify the likely customer, business model, and go-to-market motion
- check whether the role fits the user's constraints
- surface risks and open questions
- produce a practical next action

What the private skill handles:

- defines which sources to check first
- forces current-fact verification when the information may have changed
- separates confirmed facts from inference
- maps company context to user-specific role fit
- produces a decision-oriented brief instead of a generic overview

Public takeaway:

This skill turns research from passive summarization into decision support.

## 3. Outreach Drafting

Problem:

Cold outreach fails when it sounds generic. But writing a thoughtful message from scratch takes time, especially when each message needs to reflect the person's background, the company context, and the user's actual reason for reaching out.

Why a skill made sense:

The recurring job was not "write a nice email." The real job was:

- identify the most credible reason for the outreach
- keep the message short and low-friction
- avoid fake familiarity
- preserve the user's voice
- make the ask specific enough to answer

What the private skill handles:

- combines person context, company context, and user intent
- filters out weak personalization
- keeps the message concise
- avoids overclaiming shared interests or connections
- produces variants only when they are actually useful

Public takeaway:

This skill makes outreach more repeatable while keeping it human.

## 4. Resume Positioning

Problem:

AI resume rewriting often fails in two opposite ways. It either fabricates impressive claims that cannot be defended, or it becomes so conservative that the resume no longer gets interviews.

Why a skill made sense:

The recurring job was not "rewrite this bullet." The real job was:

- understand what the target role actually values
- translate messy experience into role-relevant signals
- distinguish evidence-backed claims from aggressive but defensible claims
- identify what the user needs to prepare for in interviews
- keep the final output concise enough for a real resume

What the private skill handles:

- loads role-specific standards only when relevant
- checks whether acronyms, internal names, or industry terms need explanation
- produces primary and backup positioning options
- flags claims that need interview defense
- keeps the working material private

Public takeaway:

This skill is about claim calibration. The goal is not to make the resume sound louder; it is to make strong positioning defensible.
